RECOMMENDATIONS

Your recommendations for Level 2 - Compliant

  • Commission a dedicated cybersecurity briefing for your senior leadership team. Keep it focused on business impact and personal accountability — not technical detail. A 60-90 minute session with an external facilitator carries more weight than an internal one.

  • Run a baseline human-risk assessment so you have a measurable starting point. Survey staff on awareness, run a controlled phishing simulation, and capture the results — these become the benchmark every future improvement is measured against.

  • Identify the compliance and regulatory requirements that apply to your industry and jurisdiction (Privacy Act, Essential Eight, ISO 27001, GDPR, etc.). Map each obligation to an owner inside the business so nothing sits unassigned.

  • Secure leadership buy-in by giving the executive team a one-page summary of the current human-risk posture and what an improved program would look like in 90 days. Ask for explicit sponsorship — a named exec, a budget line, and time on the agenda.

  • Communicate early wins across the organisation — phishing-report counts going up, time-to-report going down, training completion rates climbing. Visible progress in the first 60 days is what keeps momentum and budget alive.

  • Create a starter plan that covers the next 90 days: who will be trained, on what topics, how often, and how success will be measured. Keep it on a single page so it stays usable rather than becoming shelfware.

  • Assign a named program owner who is accountable for cybersecurity culture — not just the CISO or IT Manager. This person owns the roadmap, the metrics, and the conversations with leadership about progress and obstacles.

The Psyber Stages

Description

At this stage, cybersecurity awareness is non-existent. There’s no training, no ownership, and no understanding that people are part of the organisation’s defence. Security is seen purely as an IT issue, not a business, cultural, or leadership responsibility.

Impact

Organisations at this level face extreme exposure.

Without awareness, employees can’t recognise or respond to threats,making them easy targets for phishing, data loss, or social-engineering attacks. Leaders lack visibility of human-risk exposure, and compliance obligations are often missed. One mistake can have a business-wide impact.

Program Indicators

  • No awareness training or program in place.
  • No dedicated owner, budget, or plan for human-risk management.
  • Cybersecurity is not discussed at a leadership, team, or cultural level.

People Indicators

  • Employees do not talk about or consider cybersecurity in daily work.
  • Phishing or social-engineering attempts go unreported.
  • Policies are unknown, unused, or seen as blockers to productivity.
  • Staff don’t understand how their actions affect the organisation’s security.

Steps to the Next Level (→ Interested)

To move from Unaware to Interested, the goal is to establish visibility and accountability. Start small but make it official.
  • Assign a program owner: Nominate someone responsible for cybersecurity awareness, even at an ad-hoc basis.
  • Secure leadership buy-in: Have executives endorse the need to reduce human risk, publicly and visibly.
  • Identify compliance and regulatory requirements: Understand your minimum obligations and risks.
  • Run a baseline assessment: Identify where awareness gaps and behaviours pose the greatest risk.
  • Create a starter plan: Develop a basic awareness roadmap with goals, milestones, and simple metrics (e.g., training completion rates, phishing reporting rates).
  • Communicate early wins: Share stories and data that highlight the impact of awareness, building momentum for future investment.
